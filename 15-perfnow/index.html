2 · The problem is the transition

Too many options

IPv4 or IPv6
TCP + TLS or QUIC
HTTP/1.1 or HTTP/2 or HTTP/3
DNS A or AAAA or HTTPS record

Which one works depends on the user's network. The site cannot know.

3 · Let the browser race

Offer on the server, race on the client

Happy Eyeballs: the browser tries several options and keeps the first that connects.

  • 2012: IPv6, with IPv4 as a fallback (RFC 6555, then RFC 8305).
  • Now: HTTP/3 too, with HTTP/2 as a fallback (Happy Eyeballs v3, IETF draft).
  • Firefox: on in Nightly, on for 50% of Release.

Offer HTTP/3 without risk: where it breaks, the browser falls back.

4 · Make your site ready

Make your site ready for racing

  • Serve HTTP/3, and keep sending Alt-Svc.
  • Publish an HTTPS record with alpn="h3":
example.com. 3600 IN HTTPS 1 . alpn="h3,h2"
  • Give it a long lifetime, so browsers have it cached.
  • Serve IPv6 too (an AAAA record).
