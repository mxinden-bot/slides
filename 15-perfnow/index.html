2 · Rolling out new protocols

IPv6: standardised in 1998, a quarter of requests today

IPv6 was standardised in 1998. Today about a quarter of Firefox requests use it. Google will tell you half of its users reach it over IPv6, but that only counts clients reaching a server that supports it. GitHub, for example, still has no IPv6 address. There was a transition path, but it was too complex, and the internet routed around it: much of the routing moved up to HTTP. A CDN can serve millions of sites from a single IP address.

2 · Rolling out new protocols

TLS 1.3 worked: 88% after 8 years

TLS 1.3 is the opposite of IPv6: a client offers it, a server that supports it gains right away, and everyone else falls back to TLS 1.2. Eight years later it is almost 90% of Firefox handshakes.

2 · Rolling out new protocols

The internet cannot switch all at once

“Transition is easiest when changing only one entity still benefits that entity.” RFC 8170, “Planning for Protocol Adoption and Subsequent Transitions”, Internet Architecture Board, 2017

The internet is too complex to plan a transition across every network and every vendor. No one sets a date, no one is forced to switch. The IAB wrote this down in RFC 8170. And it says why IPv6 is slow: for content providers and ISPs there was no incremental benefit to deploying locally. TLS 1.3 had one. How can we do the same for HTTP/3?

2 · Rolling out new protocols

HTTP/3: about 30% of requests

HTTP/3 is about 30%, and growing slowly. Unlike IPv6, it has a clear reason to exist: it is faster, as we just saw.

3 · Offer on the server, race on the client

Happy Eyeballs: try several, keep the first that connects

  • 2012: IPv6, with IPv4 as a fallback (RFC 6555, then RFC 8305).
  • Now: HTTP/3 too, with HTTP/2 as a fallback (Happy Eyeballs v3, IETF draft).
  • Firefox: on in Nightly, on for 50% of Release.

Offer HTTP/3 without risk: where it breaks, the browser falls back.

This is the whole strategy. HTTP/3 is faster. Alt-Svc and HTTPS records are an easy way to offer it. The browser races and falls back. Servers only need to offer it, and everyone gets the benefit.

4 · Offer on the server

Give Happy Eyeballs both signals

  • Serve HTTP/3 and send an Alt-Svc header.
  • Publish an HTTPS record with alpn="h3":
example.com. 3600 IN HTTPS 1 . alpn="h3,h2"
  • Give it a long lifetime, so browsers have it cached.
